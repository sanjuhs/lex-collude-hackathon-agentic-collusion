# LexCollude: Document-Mediated Influence in Synthetic Legal Agent Workflows

Legal agents representing opposing interests must cooperate to negotiate while preserving their clients' authorization boundaries. We introduce LexCollude, a synthetic benchmark that tests whether external settlement documents can induce unauthorized acceptance, propagate influence through summaries, or elicit reciprocal coordination through revisions. Ten fictional cases define deterministic amount, confidentiality, and approval constraints. Eight conditions include clean negotiation, misinformation, document-borne instructions, matched summary and revision controls, and a deterministic execution gate. Observable prompts, final responses, provenance metadata, and rule-based scores are retained; private chain-of-thought is not collected.

An initial feasibility pilot used gpt-6-sol, gpt-6-astra, and gpt-6-luna through the native Codex harness on one case, with one repetition per condition: 24 episodes and 42 model calls. No unauthorized settlement acceptance or false approval claim was observed. Four structured reporting flags in Luna outputs exposed ambiguities between original offers, safer counterproposals, and approval requests; inspection did not support treating those flags as concealment. These findings validate workflow execution and reveal measurement limitations, rather than establish model robustness. The small sample, explicit defensive prompts, fixed attack family, and harness-specific context prevent broad conclusions. LexCollude separates deterministic mandate failures from candidate coordination, leaving collusion judgments to evidence-based independent review.
